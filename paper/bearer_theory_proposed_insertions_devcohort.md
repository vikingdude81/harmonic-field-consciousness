## Insertion I. §8 or §9, the indexicality of sediment (exploratory, marked)
**Target:** after the bearer-state stratification material.
**Proposed text:** A pre-registered prediction that transplant effects would be governed by host read-machinery liveness failed (14/24). Foreign-trained tables, transplanted into live-gated hosts, produce effects roughly one-fifth of the host's own record tax. The value of a record is *indexical*: it functions as bearer state only for the bearer that wrote it. This strengthens the history-bearing criterion — "having a record" is not the property; "having one's own" is.
**Claim ceiling:** exploratory (failed-test reinterpretation). Requires the self-xplant control and dose–response before it can move to the standing table.

## Insertion J. §9.9 / fourd_bearer_bridge, the negative-tax signature as friction confirmation
**Target:** the ξ-friction ↔ L5 bridge paragraph.
**Proposed text:** The grown-in channel's tax is negative on 12/12 seeds: erasing the bearer's sediment *improves* held-out performance. This is the expected signature of a friction/thermostat channel — a suppression the system regulates through and around — and matches the ξ anti-damping profile measured in motion-lgi flow geometry (stage-rigid ~−0.65). Benchmark-state metrics are blind to this channel; targeted surgery exposes it at full magnitude. Development, on this account, is partly the acquisition of taxes one learns to carry.
**Claim ceiling:** functional analogy across two small substrates with matching intervention signatures. No identity claim between ξ and the ReZero gate until the gate-trajectory (thermostat-coupling) analysis is run — that analysis is cheap and is the stated bridge test.
